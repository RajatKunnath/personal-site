It was an unusually hot day and I was returning from my college. The bus back home is nothing short of a nightmare for the traffic in Bangalore got worse by the minute. Watching all the two wheelers trying to squeeze in through the cars and other big vehichles, got me thinking about the world around me. All the wars, tariffs, protests, and the one that bears most of the brunt, the common man or the middle class. It would not be an exaggeration to say that we are truly living in dark times. You may think that I'm hallucinating, but honestly look around you, look at those fights outside, look at the trees, the animals, the children... Where's the innocence? Where did that fo? I asked myself. To which, my overthinking brain had no answer. Why am I at this age even thinking about all these things?? Shouldn't I be thinking of something else?
Still no answer.

It was at this moment, I saw a man making jalebis and samosas by the roadside. He was happily serving those orange delicacies to his customers which was mainly composed of migrant labourers. Our bus was stuck there in that traffic for a good 20 minutes. I patiently watched him make those jalebis. He would pour the batter in a truly artistic manner with machine like precision to that kadai with boiling hot oil. After waiting for about 2-3 mins, he would collect all the jalebis with a ladle and serve it hto and fresh. Unlike other streetside dhabhas, his was so small, that he probably couldn't afford to buy plates. He uses a piece of the newspaper as his plate. While all his customers were busy munching onto their jalebis, a young boy showed up. He was walking barefoot, wearing a worn out vest, and a pair of trousers, perhaps of 12 or so years. He pointed at those jalebis, the jalebi wallah, quickly pulled out a hot jalebi and gave it to the boy.
