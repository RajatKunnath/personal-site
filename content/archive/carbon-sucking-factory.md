## The Solution

2 companies named 'Clime Works' (a swiss company) and an Icelandic company name 'Carbfix' built a carbon capture plant in Iceland named Orca. Orka in Icelandic means Energy. Orca is a new carbon capture facility that can suck up to 4000 tons of Carbon every year.

![Orca. Image Credit - Bloomberg](/archive/img/c15a24_b3eb644343a74a1eb56dbdae1603e940-mv2.jpg)
*Orca. Image Credit - Bloomberg*

According to the UN carbon capture plants like Orca are very much required for the world to be carbon neutral by 2050. As of now Orca is the largest carbon capture facility. It took almost 18 months to complete building the plant and it has been running for the past 3 weeks.

## How Does it Work?

Orca in simple terms, is a carbon sucking factory. The process is as follows :-

1. First, there is a fan that draws air into the collector.
2. With the help of a filter, all the air is let out except for carbon.
3. Then, the collector is closed and the temperature is raised, in order to get the purest form of carbon.
4. After that, the carbon is *mineralized.
5. Voila! The Carbon is now completely defenestrated.

![](/archive/img/c15a24_eb6d6c97324a49238c680534d0302315-mv2.jpg)
*The fans in Orca. Image Credit - Smithsonian Magazine*

*Mineralization means to trap carbon into rocks so that the carbon doesn't escape out to the environment. It is a natural chemical reaction but can be sped up artificially.

Most of the rocks used for mineralization are either Igneous or Metamorphic. Some of the rocks which can be used for mineralization are- Basalt and Carbonate. You can learn more about mineralization by checking out [this website](https://www.usgs.gov/news/making-minerals-how-growing-rocks-can-help-reduce-carbon-emissions).

2. It is the biggest carbon capture plant as of now, which is true but there are only 2 facilities like Orca
3. It is stupendously expensive to run and maintain a plant like Orca. Christoph Gebald, Climeworks' co-founder, told the Washington Post that it costs at least $600 to capture one metric ton of carbon dioxide, since super-heating the air takes a lot of energy.
